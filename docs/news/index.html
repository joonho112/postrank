postrank 1.2.0

Multidomain profile report cards

  • postrank_mdomain_input() retains identified unit-by-domain states, declared target laws, structural identities and actual coupling/sampling provenance.
  • postrank_mdomain_decision() specifies weights, withholding loss, mean guards, direct joint exceedance thresholds and scopes using exact policy values.
  • postrank_mdomain_evaluate() evaluates one fixed grade matrix, and postrank_mdomain_grades() extracts a verified evaluated or selected matrix.
  • postrank_mdomain_family() constructs explicit or ordered-cut product banks; postrank_mdomain_solve() examines the complete declared bank with hard workload caps. Public selection uses additive risk A and direct joint protection. Optimality is restricted to that bank.
  • postrank_mdomain_assess() separates complete finite-law evaluation, conditional fixed-sample iid count assurance and descriptive MCMC diagnostics. Producer declarations remain declarations; no model-calibration certificate is inferred. Exact represented-measure arithmetic is distinct from numerical assurance about an intended posterior.
  • postrank_plot_mdomain() displays stored grades, cell counts or pair directions. Filtering/labels preserve original grades and denominators. Stored plots need neither a solver nor optional exact-arithmetic tooling.
  • The quickstart and decision vignettes explain separable domain decisions, dependence-sensitive protection, crossed priorities and the permitted synthetic 40-site illustration. Raw posterior archives are not bundled.

Compatibility

  • The existing 97 exports, 127 S3 registrations, P1 signatures, eight datasets and common-grade behavior are retained. Eight exports and 15 stored-summary methods are added; the package now has 105 exports and 142 S3 registrations.
  • P1 get_profile() summarizes criterion contributions to a common grading. P2 uses a unit-by-domain grade matrix; it does not replace that accessor.
  • Original prepared P1 results retain their computation versions, source IDs, backend records and timestamps. A separately sealed, exact-version reader compatibility receipt records their validation by the new reader.
  • Numeric policy inputs mean their stored binary values. Use decimal/fraction strings when the scientific policy is an exact decimal or fraction. Numeric weights retain the existing normalization rule when bound to an input.
  • Existing P1 solver defaults are preserved. P2 finite-bank evaluation and selection use optional gmp arithmetic and require no MIP backend.

postrank 1.1.0

  • Remove the 13 pilot simulation summary objects, their dedicated article and associated extraction, documentation and tests. The package retains eight example and case-study datasets and 18 vignettes. Statistical functions and retained inputs and stored analyses are unchanged.

This version revises the prose and presentation of the README, vignettes and reference documentation. The statistical definitions, supported computations, public interfaces and case-study data are unchanged from version 1.0.0. The public distribution includes the synthetic examples, stored simulation summaries and all three public-source case studies. Source-specific attribution and data conditions accompany CDE, KRW and Alabama. Documentation and data inventories describe this distribution.

postrank 1.0.0

  • Tie-aware fits now qualify a raw optimal status when a usable whole-problem lower bound is missing or the positive risk gap exceeds the requested precision. Valid grades, valid loose bounds and raw status are retained. Relative controls use the tie-aware R0-centered objective; existing tiny inversion warnings and other termination statuses retain their meanings.

  • README and the package introduction begin with one criterion, then add a second for the same six sites. Applied and Methods tracks, a migration guide and a gallery organize the documentation. The ivory/teal sticker appears in the README and website.

  • Six methodological guides explain scalar loss and the KRW correspondence, several criteria, posterior information, genuine ties, computation and Monte Carlo diagnostics. The thirteen new Applied/Methods guides link to each other and render mathematical expressions without remote scripts.

  • Seven applied guides cover a first one-criterion analysis, its extension to several criteria, the three case studies, posterior inputs, exports and sensitivity figures. The new synthetic pr_tutorial keeps the same six sites and first-criterion law when adding a second criterion. Default article builds read prepared grade results; SVG export uses optional svglite.

  • postrank_plot_pairwise() shows prepared directional-error probabilities by criterion without computing new probabilities or grades. Report cards now spread a bounded display selection across grades, accept display names and facet layouts, and require one criterion for mean ordering. Probability axes, missing frontier segments and method-status displays are clearer.

  • as.data.frame() on an analysis now returns a stable ID/grade table; request what = "report_card" for the prepared richer table. Console summaries and tie-aware views prioritize results and retain technical details on request.

  • Progress defaults to quiet; postrank_control(verbose = NULL) inherits the option at solve time, while an explicit TRUE/FALSE takes precedence.

  • Three compact case-study inputs retain their source laws, directions and archived-result qualifications. CDE source attribution, the KRW source’s CC-BY-NC notice and Alabama’s CC BY 4.0 attribution are preserved. The scoped LICENSE and installed DATA-NOTICE distinguish the MIT code license from third-party data conditions.

  • postrank_probabilities() accepts previously calculated tie-free pairwise probabilities for one or several criteria. validate_postrank_probabilities() checks the stored input, corrections and optional supplied marginal summaries. The initial scope is weighted expected loss with an at-most-G limit.

  • A single-criterion analysis can omit its weight, which is one. Several criteria still require declared loss weights.

  • A backend optimal status is qualified as feasible but uncertified when its recorded lower bound and gap do not support the requested precision. Raw status, independently validated grades and usable bounds are preserved.

postrank 0.9.0

GitHub release candidate. The reviewed mathematical kernels and the legacy scientific payload identities are preserved; every inherited behavioral case remains an obligation, with reviewed public-adapter and English presentation expectation migrations. Acceptance of the new API precedes any 1.0.0 label.

Opt-in tie-aware mode (development addition)

  • The separate tie_aware_v1 domain adds as_postrank_tie_draws(), validate_postrank_tie_draws(), read_postrank_tie_draws() and write_postrank_tie_draws() for explicitly declared genuine ties in joint IID/MCMC inputs or complete exact finite laws. Legacy schemas and readers remain unchanged. No sampled coincidence establishes structural identity.
  • postrank_tie_pairwise() and validate_postrank_tie_pairwise() retain strict reversal, equality and distinct probabilities, their source and separate content seals. Exact finite laws retain integer-mass/rational metadata.
  • postrank_tie_problem(), postrank_tie_evaluate(), postrank_tie_solve() and validate_postrank_tie_solution() implement weighted at-most-G with a mandatory full active-profile same-grade guard. The label-MIP uses pair-specific withholding costs and the all-withhold offset R0. All original entities and pair multiplicities are preserved. Zero-tie loss reduces mathematically to the legacy loss; calculations remain subject to floating-point arithmetic.
  • postrank_tie_capabilities() records the narrow initial boundary. Staged solving, q, minimax, exact-G, typed analytic inputs, legacy extras and uniform guarantees are unsupported. New classes never inherit legacy input, pairwise, problem or fit classes. Existing 0.9 defaults, behavioral cases, golden hashes, public signatures and cached assets retain their original meaning.

One-call analysis, strict inputs and settings

  • postrank() builds the pairwise coefficients, solves, validates the incumbent and attaches the default extras (report card on; Monte Carlo fragility for joint IID or MCMC input; cross-fitting and diagnostics off), recording why each extra is or is not available.
  • postrank_draws() is a bookkeeping front end for arrays, criterionwise matrix lists, posterior draws and existing objects; it always ends in the strict constructors. postrank_declarations() bundles the eight required scientific declarations, which have no defaults. The bundle and friendly array route reject generic placeholder tokens; direct legacy constructors, canonical JSON and metadata routes retain their existing declaration and structural checks. No route verifies scientific truth.
  • postrank_settings() (policy: weights, tau, G_max, optional modes) and postrank_control() (computation) are separate typed objects; loose arguments that conflict with settings raise settings_conflict.
  • The 0.0.1.9003 typed coefficients are ported: postrank_gaussian_coefficients(), postrank_mixture_coefficients(), validate_postrank_coefficients(), postrank_coefficient_capabilities() and postrank_coefficient_diagnose().

Baselines, comparisons and grids

  • postrank_posterior_means(), postrank_expected_rank() and postrank_composite() give baseline scores; postrank_cuts() cuts a score order optimally (exact dynamic program) or by quantiles under the same loss.
  • postrank_compare() puts the joint rule and the baselines on one posterior and policy, keeping status, optimality scope and reason separate. Its table now records family_feasible and feasibility_reason using the reference fit’s stored feasibility tolerance. Supplied actions outside the declared family keep their values and relations and are marked in views; old objects without the metadata are labeled as not recorded. Duplicate method labels receive collision-free IDs throughout the result.
  • postrank_settings_grid() and postrank_sensitivity() run declared settings grids. Losses or feasible sets may change between rows, so risks are compared only within one loss. Profile names such as base are ordinary labels; distinct policy values retain distinct display labels. Invalid fractional grade caps and malformed named axes are rejected before coercion.
  • postrank_composite() matches named scales and centers by criterion ID; named vectors must cover every criterion exactly once. Use an unnamed scalar for recycling. Quantile cuts must satisfy the declared G_max and exact_G after exact-score blocks are preserved.

Reporting, accessors and console

  • postrank_report_card() lists each entity’s grade, its disclosed relation counts and its posterior means and central intervals. Mixture interval inversion uses standardized coordinates and verifies the probability residual after rounding to target units; unrepresentable endpoints raise posterior_quantile_failure instead of returning an inaccurate interval.
  • get_grades(), get_relations(), get_profile() and get_risk() read results from analyses, fits and baselines.
  • format(x, width = 72, tier = 2) is the preferred capture interface across result classes. The 24 named formatter wrappers return the same lines as format() with the same arguments; none is deprecated in this release.
  • Every printed class has a result-first English console view, with exported capture formatters: format_postrank_analysis_cli(), format_postrank_backend_availability_cli(), format_postrank_baseline_cli(), format_postrank_coefficient_diagnostics_cli(), format_postrank_coefficients_cli(), format_postrank_commonality_cost_cli(), format_postrank_comparison_cli(), format_postrank_control_cli(), format_postrank_crossfit_cli(), format_postrank_declarations_cli(), format_postrank_diagnostics_cli(), format_postrank_draws_cli(), format_postrank_fit_cli(), format_postrank_frontier_cli(), format_postrank_guarantee_cli(), format_postrank_mc_fragility_cli(), format_postrank_pairwise_cli(), format_postrank_problem_cli(), format_postrank_report_card_cli(), format_postrank_report_cli(), format_postrank_sensitivity_cli(), format_postrank_settings_cli(), format_postrank_summary_cli() and format_postrank_validation_cli().
  • postrank_report() writes a standalone English HTML report with eleven sections and embeds the report-card and criterion-profile figures as base64 PNG images when they are available; it never overwrites an existing file. Prepared coefficient diagnostics are accepted with schema/source checks, conditional-risk scope and explicit unavailable metric reasons. Reports do not recompute risks, diagnostics or report cards.

Figures

  • Six shipped ggplot2 figures: postrank_plot_report_card(), postrank_plot_relations(), postrank_plot_profile(), postrank_plot_frontier(), postrank_plot_commonality() and postrank_plot_compare(), with postrank_plot_data() for their data, autoplot() and plot() methods for seven classes, theme_postrank(), postrank_palette(), scale_colour_grade(), scale_color_grade() and scale_fill_grade(). Views read stored results and never solve.

Data and documentation

  • Seventeen bundled data objects: the 32-state toy, a sealed 4,000-draw example (pr_example_draws) with its known law as Gaussian coefficients (pr_example_gaussian), the two-state MCMC diagnostic toy (pr_toy_mcmc), ten pilot summary tables, two pilot lookups and pilot_headline.
  • Four vignettes: a seven-step quickstart, strict inputs and producers, baselines and criterion burden, and the pilot evidence with its claim limits. Ordinary builds read sealed cached results and solve nothing. The 0.0.1.9002 guide is retired; its chunks moved into these articles.
  • Core return schemas, solver-free evaluation/diagnostic recipes and route validation contracts are documented. The installed pilot source register identifies every headline/claim-limit source ID and explicitly marks the original sources as not publicly available; summary redraw is distinct from regeneration of the full pilot experiment.

Intentional compatibility changes

  • Backend resolution is explicit argument, then getOption("postrank.backend"), then "gurobi", for every solving verb. An unavailable backend is an error with remedies; there is no fallback.
  • Official output is English only: lang = "ko" (or any other language) raises unsupported_language, and print() on a fit no longer writes a report.
  • cmdstanr is no longer suggested: no installed code calls Stan.
  • The help pages of the inherited and ported functions are generated from roxygen; their tests are self-contained.

Evidence and limitations

  • Solver bounds are numerical bounds, not certificates or unique-optimum claims; a zero gap does not enumerate other optima.
  • The pilot tables describe a finite simulation roster; they are not evidence of general superiority of any rule.
  • The continuous-integration workflow installs HiGHS and excludes the optional non-CRAN Gurobi package from dependency resolution, so it does not validate the Gurobi backend.
  • A constructor for arbitrary user-supplied coefficient arrays is deferred.

Main-study candidate (never released or checked on its own). This entry was written for 0.9.0, which ports the candidate’s coefficient API.

New features

  • Typed conditional-law coefficients: postrank_gaussian_coefficients(), postrank_mixture_coefficients(), validate_postrank_coefficients(), postrank_coefficient_capabilities() and postrank_coefficient_diagnose(). These artifacts carry their own content identity and capability record and never fabricate target draws or sample sizes.

postrank 0.0.1.9002

Development version with independent audit corrections and an LP time-budget repair.

Audit corrections

  • AUD-01: minimax refinement preserves the original weighted secondary objective. Zero weights no longer trigger a perturbation of that objective. A separate Pareto tie-break, when applicable, preserves the preceding objectives, and certification describes the action actually released.
  • AUD-02: cross-fit unbiasedness is conditional on independent IID folds and a linear weighted or criterion-specific risk functional. Empirical minimax maxima and MCMC fold comparisons are descriptive; no exact unbiasedness guarantee is claimed for them. Numerical cross-fit summaries are unchanged.
  • Regression tests include the zero/tiny-weight minimax counterexample and exact enumeration of the minimax cross-fit bias example.

postrank 0.0.1.9001

Development version with an LP time-budget repair following version 0.0.1.9000.

Changes that affect existing code

  • When the staged engine’s MIP stage cannot run (control$engine$mip = "never", or "auto" with more than mip_max_n entities, 200 by default), the LP relaxation now gets the remaining time less a reserve, and never less than lp_time_share of it. The reserve covers the incumbent candidates built from the relaxation’s solution and the final validation; it is the largest of 1 second, 1% of time_limit and twice the time the incumbent stage took. Before, the relaxation got only lp_time_share (0.5), so these runs stopped at about half of control$time_limit. When the relaxation does not close the gap first, they now use nearly all of it: lower bounds can be tighter, and wall time roughly doubles (also for postrank_frontier(), postrank_commonality_cost() and postrank_crossfit() on such problems).
  • With the default minimax second stage, stage 1 now uses nearly all of its three quarters of the time limit on such problems, so stage 2 gets about the last quarter instead of the larger remainder it had before (n = 250 with a 60 s limit: 15 s instead of 34 s; the whole run took 59.5 s instead of 45.3 s).
  • lp_time_share sets the relaxation’s share when the MIP stage may follow; otherwise it is only a lower bound on its budget. fit$engine$stages$lp records the budget the relaxation received (time_budget) and the rule that set it (time_budget_rule); each round in its trace records the solver time limit for that call (time_limit).
  • The HiGHS LP stage resets the solver’s time limit before each call to the run time used so far plus the remaining budget. HiGHS counts the limit against the solver object’s cumulative run time, so the cut separation between calls was never charged and the stage could overrun its budget (in the weighted and q runs of the 014 Phase 2 benchmark, by 10-13 s at n = 400 and 74-88 s at n = 807). A call that still runs more than a second past the budget adds a warning. Gurobi already received the remaining budget on each call.
  • Without a MIP stage, the LP stage separates no more cuts once its budget is spent; they could only have served a MIP stage.

postrank 0.0.1.9000

Development version following a review of the solver and validation routines.

Changes that affect existing code

  • postrank_solve() gains strategy = c("staged", "label_mip"). The default "staged" engine builds incumbents by an order dynamic program and local search, bounds them with a weak-order LP, and calls a strengthened label MIP only when a gap remains on small problems. "label_mip" keeps the 0.0.1 formulation.
  • Minimax problems are solved in two stages by default (postrank_problem(minimax_refine = "weighted")). Stage 1 receives three quarters of control$time_limit, and fit$backend$options records stage 1’s options; fit$minimax_refine$time_limits records the budgets of both stages. minimax_refine = "none" returns the stage-1 incumbent.
  • fit$objective$risk_upper_bound is the risk of the released action (for minimax, the largest selected criterion risk of the grade). The solver’s epigraph value is reported separately as fit$objective$epigraph_value.
  • fit$objective$raw_bound_minus_recomputed_incumbent and risk_bound_minus_recomputed_incumbent are renamed raw_bound_minus_action_raw and risk_bound_minus_action_risk.
  • A reported lower bound above the validated action risk by at most the scale-aware objective tolerance is clamped to that risk (gap 0; the bound_source ends in _clamped) instead of being discarded. Larger inversions remain numerical issues, and a clamped inversion larger than the gap controls (absolute, or relative on the raw objective) adds a warning.
  • An infeasible fit reports no lower bound (bound_source = "missing") from either strategy or backend; the backend’s raw value stays in fit$objective$raw_lower_bound.
  • Lower-bound source labels are checked against a fixed list; an unknown label, an infeasible status with a validated incumbent, or an excessive raw primal residual is recorded as a numerical issue and the bound is not used.
  • Gurobi and HiGHS receive half the validator’s feasibility and integrality tolerances. HiGHS mip_feasibility_tolerance is tied to the requested absolute risk gap.

New functions

  • postrank_draws_from_array() builds a validated draws object from an S x n x K array; the scientific declarations remain required arguments, and for MCMC so is the chain layout (chains, warmup_per_chain, thin).
  • postrank_crossfit() and postrank_mc_fragility() are practical Monte Carlo diagnostics: out-of-fold optimism and pairs whose decision margin is within a few Monte Carlo standard errors of tau.
  • postrank_commonality_cost() reports, per criterion, the extra risk of using one common grade; postrank_frontier() solves along a grid of tau or q.

Other changes

  • Observed within-criterion ties are still rejected; the message now names the usual cause (reduced output precision) and the fix (re-export at full precision).
  • Minimax problems record degenerate criteria (problem$minimax_diagnostics), and fits warn about them.
  • The Gurobi model conversion is vectorized.
  • The regret consistency check compares risk differences in risk units.
  • inst/CITATION cites Kline, Rose and Walters (2024); the README and vignette cover the staged engine, the minimax second stage and the new tools.
