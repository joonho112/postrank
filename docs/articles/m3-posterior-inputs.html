Posterior inputs and pairwise probabilities

Source: vignettes/m3-posterior-inputs.Rmd
m3-posterior-inputs.Rmd

The posterior representation required for a grading analysis depends on the functional of interest. Pairwise comparison probabilities determine additive expected loss, but generally do not determine composite ordering, simultaneous correctness or the distribution of realized loss. We examine these information requirements for draws, finite laws, Gaussian laws, mixtures and supplied numerical probabilities.

The examples use enumeration of small specified laws and numerical integration of a normal comparison; they require neither a fitted model nor an optimizer. Analysis of supplied posterior results gives executable declarations and array constructions. The mathematical basis is developed in the companion theory text’s treatment of joint laws, pair-coefficient risk and coefficient error.1

Directional-error probabilities

Let θi\theta_i be an entity’s target, oriented so that larger values mean higher reporting priority. All probabilities below condition on the observed information and the supplied model; we suppress that conditioning to keep the notation readable. For distinct entities define

eij=Pr⁡(θi≤θj). e_{ij}=\Pr(\theta_i\leq\theta_j).

This is the expected error cost of placing i above j. It is neither a distance between their posterior means nor the probability that a fitted coefficient is positive. In the tie-free case, eij+eji=1e_{ij}+e_{ji}=1. In general the sum is 1+Pr⁡(θi=θj)1+\Pr(\theta_i=\theta_j). Continuous marginal distributions alone do not guarantee tie freedom: if both targets equal the same continuous random variable, equality has probability one.

The package stores NA on the diagonal because self-comparisons are excluded. It does not store the mathematical probability Pr⁡(θi≤θi)=1\Pr(\theta_i\leq\theta_i)=1 there. Ordinary draw, finite-law, Gaussian, mixture, and public probability inputs discussed here use the tie-free domain. Genuine equality mass belongs to the separately declared domain in True ties and withheld comparisons.

For a fixed report gg, grade 1 has highest priority. Write xij(g)=𝟏{gi<gj}x_{ij}(g)=\mathbf1\{g_i<g_j\}, M=(n2)M=\binom n2, and N(g)=∑i≠jxij(g)N(g)=\sum_{i\ne j}x_{ij}(g). Each disclosed pair contributes once, in its reported direction. Withholding cost τ\tau gives expected loss

R(g)=∑i≠jxij(g)eij+τ{M−N(g)}M. R(g)=\frac{\sum_{i\ne j}x_{ij}(g)e_{ij} +\tau\{M-N(g)\}}{M}.

The identity follows by taking expectations of the wrong-direction indicators. Independence between the error events is unnecessary, so the coefficients determine the distributional information required for this expectation. This result concerns a particular loss functional and does not establish statistical sufficiency for the posterior law. The feasible reports and decision settings are specified separately; see Posterior ranking, grades, and loss.

Dependence and pairwise ordering

The following two joint laws give each listed state probability one third. They are the dependence counterexample in the theory text’s “Dependence and pairwise comparison probabilities” section.

couplings <- list(
  Q1 = cbind(A = c(0, 1, 2), B = c(1, 2, 0)),
  Q2 = cbind(A = c(0, 1, 2), B = c(2, 0, 1))
)
coupling_table <- do.call(rbind, lapply(names(couplings), function(name) {
  z <- couplings[[name]]
  data.frame(law = name,
    states = paste(sprintf("(%s, %s)", z[, "A"], z[, "B"]), collapse = "; "),
    mean_A = mean(z[, "A"]), mean_B = mean(z[, "B"]),
    error_A_above_B = mean(z[, "A"] <= z[, "B"]))
}))
knitr::kable(coupling_table, digits = 3, row.names = FALSE,
  col.names = c("Law", "States (A, B)", "Mean A", "Mean B", "Error: A above B"))
Law States (A, B) Mean A Mean B Error: A above B
Q1 (0, 1); (1, 2); (2, 0) 1 1 0.667
Q2 (0, 2); (1, 0); (2, 1) 1 1 0.333

Under both laws each entity is uniform on {0,1,2}\{0,1,2\}, with the same mean and variance. There are no within-state ties. Nevertheless, the error of reporting A above B is 0.667 under Q1 and 0.333 under Q2. The preferred direction reverses. Independently sampling the two marginals would specify a third law, including equality states that neither original law contains.

Joint draws must retain the entity values from each common state. Independent permutations of the entity columns preserve marginal histograms while potentially changing comparison probabilities. Named identifiers prevent coordinate misalignment, but the intended dependence must also be maintained by the procedure generating the draws.

Finite laws and Monte Carlo approximations

For a completely specified finite law, states θ(s)\theta^{(s)} and masses psp_s define the probability itself:

eij=∑sps𝟏{θi(s)≤θj(s)}. e_{ij}=\sum_s p_s\mathbf1\{\theta_i^{(s)}\leq\theta_j^{(s)}\}.

The coupling table uses that enumeration. In the package’s exact finite-law route, complete integer-valued target states and positive safe-integer mass numerators share a common denominator. as_postrank_draws() validates the canonical input, and postrank_pairwise() retains rational coefficients using the optional gmp package. Its ordinary numeric e array is a double projection. Exact rational coefficients do not certify every downstream floating-point sum or optimization result.

Monte Carlo input has a different interpretation. Equal-weight retained draws give the empirical estimate

êij=S−1∑s=1S𝟏{θi(s)≤θj(s)}. \widehat e_{ij}=S^{-1}\sum_{s=1}^S \mathbf1\{\theta_i^{(s)}\leq\theta_j^{(s)}\}.

An empirical distribution of retained draws is an approximation to the posterior, even though its stored support is finite. Estimated probabilities of zero or one can arise because relevant events were absent from the sample. IID and MCMC estimates use the same arithmetic average but require different numerical uncertainty assessments. Chain membership, original iteration coordinates, warmup and retained spacing are therefore part of the input record. Declared jointness or convergence remains an assumption to be assessed independently.

Ordinary inputs exclude observed within-criterion ties. Jittering changes the supplied values and can change the represented law; the appropriate response depends on whether full-precision values can be recovered or an equality model is required. Similarly, arbitrary importance-weighted draws are outside the equal-weight input domain.

Conditional Gaussian integration avoids target simulation

Suppose entities are independent conditional on a fixed shared state hh, and the target for entity ii has conditional normal mean mi(h)m_i(h) and variance vi(h)>0v_i(h)>0. Then

eij(h)=Φ(mj(h)−mi(h)vi(h)+vj(h)). e_{ij}(h)=\Phi\!\left( \frac{m_j(h)-m_i(h)}{\sqrt{v_i(h)+v_j(h)}}\right).

With conditional between-entity covariance cij(h)c_{ij}(h), the difference variance would instead include −2cij(h)-2c_{ij}(h). The Gaussian constructor does not accept an arbitrary between-entity covariance matrix. Its covariance array describes criteria within each entity, and the caller explicitly declares conditional independence across entities.

For the specified fictional law in pr_tutorial, the Gaussian formula can be compared with independent numerical integration. For sites A and B, the integral ∫fA(t){1−FB(t)}dt\int f_A(t)\{1-F_B(t)\}\,dt gives the probability of the same event A≤BA\leq B.

law <- pr_tutorial$law
gaussian <- postrank_gaussian_coefficients(
  law$means[, "score_A", drop = FALSE],
  law$covariance["score_A", "score_A", , drop = FALSE],
  conditional_independence = "entities_given_shared_h",
  provenance = pr_tutorial$one$provenance
)
mu <- law$means[c("A", "B"), "score_A"]
sd <- sqrt(law$covariance["score_A", "score_A", c("A", "B")])
normal_formula <- pnorm((mu["B"] - mu["A"]) / sqrt(sum(sd^2)))
quadrature <- integrate(function(t)
  dnorm(t, mu["A"], sd["A"]) *
    pnorm(t, mu["B"], sd["B"], lower.tail = FALSE),
  lower = -Inf, upper = Inf, rel.tol = 1e-10)
normal_table <- data.frame(
  calculation = c("Normal difference formula", "Numerical integral", "Package coefficients"),
  error_A_above_B = c(normal_formula, quadrature$value, gaussian$e["A", "B", 1])
)
knitr::kable(normal_table, digits = 8, format.args = list(nsmall = 8),
  row.names = FALSE, col.names = c("Calculation", "Error: A above B"))
Calculation Error: A above B
Normal difference formula 0.18837956
Numerical integral 0.18837956
Package coefficients 0.18837956

All three values round to 0.18837956. The provenance record is retained because the calculation uses exactly the dataset’s score-A law. Agreement with the formula and the integration routine’s error estimate provide a numerical check for this law. They neither justify a Gaussian approximation for another posterior nor constitute a validated enclosure for every coefficient.

Shared-state mixtures

If hsh_s has supplied mass πs\pi_s, then eij=∑sπseij(hs)e_{ij}=\sum_s\pi_s e_{ij}(h_s). postrank_mixture_coefficients() performs this conditional-probability average. Each state applies to all entities together. Independently choosing a hyperstate for each entity would change the model. Integrating over a shared state can induce dependence even though entities are independent conditional on that state.

Mixture masses πs\pi_s describe the probability law; they are unrelated to criterion-loss weights. Fixed finite mixtures accept supplied nonnegative weights summing to one. The hyperparameter-MCMC route uses uniform weights on retained states and requires their original chain and iteration records. This procedure does not simulate target draws. Its conditional-risk traces, when available, integrate out target variation within each state and differ from realized target-loss traces. A finite mixture of continuous normal laws also differs from a finite law of target atoms. Averaging moments first and using one Gaussian generally changes the comparison probabilities.

Information requirements across criteria

For criterion kk, retain eij,ke_{ij,k}. With nonnegative decision weights summing to one, replace eije_{ij} in the risk by e‾ij=∑kwkeij,k\bar e_{ij}=\sum_k w_k e_{ij,k}. Linearity of expectation needs no cross-criterion independence assumption. Criterion-wise blocks can therefore contain different numbers of draws, each preserving its own joint entity vector, sampling history, and target definition. Row numbers from separate fits do not create a common cross-criterion state.

The distinction is visible in another complete enumeration. B is fixed at zero on both criteria. In the first law A’s criteria move together; in the second they move oppositely. Each row below has mass one half.

criterion_laws <- list(
  Together = rbind(c(2, 2), c(-1, -1)),
  Opposite = rbind(c(2, -1), c(-1, 2))
)
information_table <- do.call(rbind, lapply(names(criterion_laws), function(name) {
  a <- criterion_laws[[name]]
  data.frame(law = name,
    weighted_error = mean(rowMeans(a <= 0)),
    composite_error = mean(rowMeans(a) <= 0),
    both_criteria_correct = mean(rowSums(a > 0) == 2L))
}))
knitr::kable(information_table, row.names = FALSE,
  col.names = c("Law", "Weighted error", "Composite error", "Both correct"))
Law Weighted error Composite error Both correct
Together 0.5 0.5 0.5
Opposite 0.5 0.0 0.0

For the report A above B, the equally weighted directional error is 0.5 under either law. All criterion-wise comparison probabilities agree. Yet the error for the equally weighted cardinal composite is 0.5 versus 0, and the probability of being correct on both criteria is 0.5 versus 0. Neither law has a target or composite tie. This example follows the theory text’s “Exact comparison of additive, composite, and joint-event information” section.

Pair coefficients do not determine a total-loss distribution or its variance. The current package also requires suitable joint target draws for total-loss traces and its joint-draw fragility/crossfit helpers. Some per-criterion diagnostics can use the corresponding criterion block, while scalar MCMC metrics still require adequate retained history. Mathematical availability and a function’s supported input classes are separate questions. See Several criteria and competing goals for targets and Monte Carlo uncertainty and diagnostics for the diagnostic requirements.

Supplied numerical probabilities

postrank_probabilities() accepts probabilities calculated outside the package. For example, the normal coefficients above can be supplied as directional errors:

probabilities <- postrank_probabilities(
  gaussian$e[, , "score_A"], criterion = "score_A",
  event = "directional_error", zero_ties = TRUE, margin = 0,
  source = list(kind = "analytic_reference",
    description = "Normal difference probabilities for pr_tutorial score A",
    conditioning = law$conditioning),
  targets = c(score_A = "Illustrative standardized score; larger means higher priority")
)
identical(probabilities$e, gaussian$e)
#> [1] TRUE

This route also admits numerical integration from a specified non-Gaussian law. It requires matching entity axes, NA diagonals, probabilities in [0,1][0,1], and reverse complements within tolerance. Zero equality mass and zero margin are explicit requirements. A matrix can pass these local checks without being realizable by a coherent joint posterior. For example, certainty in A above B, B above C, and C above A is incompatible with scalar targets, even though each reverse pair could sum to one.

Small complement discrepancies may be corrected only by explicitly requesting correction = "symmetric" and a tolerance. The raw values and maximum adjustment remain recorded. For a fixed report, if criterion kk’s largest adjustment is dkd_k, the risk changes by at most

N(g)M∑kwkdk. \frac{N(g)}{M}\sum_k w_k d_k.

The bound follows from the triangle inequality applied to adjustments over the disclosed pairs. It describes the difference between supplied and corrected coefficients. Error relative to the intended posterior requires justified bounds on the differences from that posterior’s probabilities. Symmetrization, grid refinement and a small optimization gap do not establish such bounds; each addresses a different numerical operation. Posterior uncertainty remains a property of the underlying law.

Representation retained Available information Information not created
Joint target draws Empirical comparisons and joint target states Proof of convergence or identification
Criterion-wise blocks Within-criterion comparisons and histories Cross-criterion joint states
Gaussian or shared-state mixture Conditional-law coefficients and source moments Target-draw sample sizes or general between-entity covariance
Supplied probabilities Weighted expected risk for the supported at-most-G problem Draw histories, joint events, or an error certificate

Direct probability inputs can carry optional means and intervals only when they describe the same oriented targets. Probabilities alone do not recover those summaries. Native joint problem construction for Gaussian, mixture, and direct-probability inputs currently supports weighted at-most-G problems. This restriction is operation specific: postrank_cuts() can also optimize an exact grade count within a fixed score order for Gaussian and mixture coefficients. Direct-probability cuts remain at-most-G. None of these paths automatically enables every mode available for draw inputs.

All these calculations are conditional on the information represented by the posterior producer. In plug-in empirical Bayes, the estimated prior is held fixed unless further uncertainty is explicitly propagated. A hierarchical mixture integrates the uncertainty represented by its states. Validation and accurate numerical integration do not, by themselves, establish calibration under a different data-generating law. Interpretation of the resulting grades therefore remains conditional on the specified posterior.
