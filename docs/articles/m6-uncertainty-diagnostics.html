Monte Carlo diagnostics, cross-fitting, and IID bounds

Source: vignettes/m6-uncertainty-diagnostics.Rmd
m6-uncertainty-diagnostics.Rmd

Three different kinds of uncertainty can arise in a posterior grading analysis:

  1. Posterior uncertainty about the entity targets θ\theta under your statistical model (for example, a 0.45 probability that A is below B).
  2. Monte Carlo approximation error from estimating pairwise probabilities and expected losses with a finite sample of simulation draws SS.
  3. Model misspecification—differences between your statistical model (or fixed empirical-Bayes prior) and the actual population.

Drawing more posterior samples shrinks Monte Carlo error without shrinking posterior uncertainty or fixing a misspecified model. This article explains postrank’s Monte Carlo diagnostics (postrank_diagnose()), an MCSE screen for borderline pairs (postrank_mc_fragility()), split-sample cross-fitting (postrank_crossfit()), and conditional fixed-sample IID bounds (postrank_iid_bounds(), postrank_regret_bound(), postrank_protection_envelopes(), postrank_protection_analysis()). Every executed chunk uses small synthetic draw datasets and stored results without model fitting or optimization.

Diagnostics are descriptive summaries, not guarantees. Monte Carlo standard errors, MCMC effective sample sizes, R-hat checks, fragility counts, and cross-fitting differences are descriptive summaries of simulation variation on your supplied draws; they do not guarantee convergence, finite-sample coverage, or model adequacy.

Pairwise Monte Carlo standard errors

On criterion k, the directional-error probability eij,k=Pr⁡(θik≤θjk)e_{ij,k} = \Pr(\theta_{ik} \le \theta_{jk}) is estimated from SS equal-weight draws by the sample average of the 0/1 indicator 𝟏{θik(s)≤θjk(s)}\mathbf{1}\{\theta_{ik}^{(s)} \le \theta_{jk}^{(s)}\}. A value near 0.5 means the model itself is uncertain about which entity ranks higher; the Monte Carlo standard error (MCSE) measures how much that sample average varies across simulation runs of size SS.

postrank_toy holds 32 independent (regime = "iid") joint draws for three fictional sites on two criteria (32 draws is tiny—used here so numerical errors are large enough to inspect). Passing a one-row pairs data frame to postrank_diagnose() computes the indicator trace and MCSE for 001 ahead of site-B on need_A:

library(postrank)

pair <- data.frame(
  entity_i = "001", entity_j = "site-B", criterion_id = "need_A"
)
iid_diagnostic <- postrank_diagnose(postrank_toy, pairs = pair)
pair_summary <- iid_diagnostic[["summaries"]][["pair_1"]]
z_iid <- iid_diagnostic[["traces"]][["pair_1"]][["values"]]
knitr::kable(data.frame(
  Draws = length(z_iid),
  Estimated_probability = mean(z_iid),
  MCSE = pair_summary[["diagnostics"]][["mcse_mean"]][["value"]],
  SD_over_sqrt_S = sd(z_iid) / sqrt(length(z_iid))
), digits = 4)

32 0.25 0.0778 0.0778

For independent draws, the MCSE equals the sample standard deviation divided by S\sqrt{S}—for a 0/1 indicator with sample mean p̂\widehat{p}, p̂(1−p̂)/(S−1)\sqrt{\widehat{p}(1 - \widehat{p}) / (S - 1)} (0.0778 here at p̂=0.25\widehat{p} = 0.25, S=32S = 32). Pointwise MCSEs describe one pair at a time; they do not form simultaneous bands across all pairs or give the posterior probability that a full grading is right. See Lee (2026), Chapter 21, Monte Carlo error under a declared sampling law.

Loss traces for a fixed grading

When you also pass a fixed integer grade vector and settings to postrank_diagnose(), it evaluates criterion-specific and weighted total losses draw by draw. On joint S x n x K draws, criteria are combined within each joint draw s, preserving cross-criterion simulation covariance rather than treating separate criterion MCSEs as independent. On postrank_toy, postrank_diagnose() returns the IID standard error of the fixed 1/2/2 grading’s weighted loss while recording convergence_verified = FALSE and finite_sample_certificate = FALSE:

pr_toy_mcmc stores four independent two-state Markov chains of 512 iterations each (S = 2,048 total, no warmup or thinning) with transition probabilities 0.012 (0 -> 1) and 0.048 (1 -> 0). Its true stationary probability of state 1 is 0.2000, with strong serial autocorrelation:

Reading MCMC diagnostics

For equal-length, regularly thinned chains, postrank_diagnose() delegates scalar chain summaries to the posterior package (rhat, ess_bulk, ess_tail, ess_mean, and mcse_mean on split chains). These are univariate summaries per trace, distinct from multivariate effective sample size; see Lee (2026), Chapter 21, Multivariate output analysis and effective sample size.

On 0/1 indicator traces, quantile indicators at 0.05 or 0.95 can be constant, producing ESS_tail = NA (recorded with available = FALSE and an explicit unavailable_reason, never silently replaced by 0). Conversely, an all-zero empirical indicator trace can simply mean a rare ordering reversal was never visited during the run. Unequal chain lengths keep all draws in pooled means without truncating or padding chains to compute cross-chain R-hat or ESS, and single-chain runs cannot check between-chain agreement.

In pr_toy_mcmc, Rhat is 1.0905 (above the 1.01 heuristic) and both ESS_mean and ess_bulk are 42.95 (below 400, i.e., 100 per chain across 4 chains), triggering three exploration warnings in diagnostic$warnings. Comparing the MCMC MCSE to a naive IID sd / sqrt(S) calculation shows why ignoring autocorrelation is misleading:

chain_means <- diagnostic[["summaries"]][["pair_1"]][["chain_means"]]

Treating the 2,048 autocorrelated draws as independent would understate the MCSE by nearly sevenfold (0.0083 vs. 0.0576). Likewise, effective sample size (ESS) cannot be plugged into an IID concentration inequality in place of SS (postrank does not issue finite-sample concentration certificates for MCMC chains).

Fixed gradings versus data-selected gradings

Evaluating postrank_diagnose() on a grade vector chosen before looking at the draws estimates the loss of that fixed grading. When you pass a solved fit instead, postrank_diagnose() checks that fit came from the same input and labels the trace summaries as post-selection descriptive summaries: optimizing grades on the same draws introduces selection optimism that pointwise MCSEs do not capture.

Note two special cases:

  • If a grading places every entity in Grade 1 (N = 0), its weighted loss is identically τ\tau on every draw (structural_constant_from_action, zero loss variance, E_over_N = NA), even if individual entity targets mix poorly.
  • Externally supplied postrank_probabilities() carry no draw history, so MCSE and ESS cannot be recovered from them; postrank_coefficient_diagnose() on hyperparameter_mcmc mixtures diagnoses integration over conditional normal expectations rather than entity-draw dispersion (see Posterior representations and pairwise comparison probabilities).

Fragility and split-sample cross-fitting

Two fast summaries in postrank() probe sensitivity to simulation noise on joint target draws:

  • Borderline-pair fragility (postrank_mc_fragility(), requested by default via fragility = TRUE). For each unordered pair, postrank compares the weighted error probability in its likelier direction (min⁡{e‾ij,e‾ji}\min\{\bar{e}_{ij}, \bar{e}_{ji}\}) against τ\tau, flagging pairs whose distance from τ\tau is strictly less than z (default 2) times that pair’s directional MCSE. Because grades are chosen jointly under transitivity and G_max, a borderline pair need not flip any grade (and a non-borderline pair can still move when neighbors shift). On MCMC inputs, only pairs strictly within screen_margin = 0.1 of τ\tau are traced for speed, recording an informational "MCMC screen" note in warnings.
  • Two-fold draw cross-fitting (postrank_crossfit(), requested via crossfit = TRUE). Splits retained draws into two folds (halves for IID; groups of whole chains for multi-chain MCMC, or halves for a single chain), optimizes a grading on each fold (requiring a solver such as highs or licensed Gurobi), and evaluates that grading on the held-out fold. Under independent IID folds and pre-fixed weighted settings, the held-out evaluation is an unbiased estimate of the fold grading’s posterior expected loss (and mean_abs_optimism avoids sign cancellation when both folds pick the same grades). Cross-fitting splits posterior draws from your existing model fit—it does not refit the statistical model on held-out observations, does not make dependent MCMC chains independent, does not apply unbiasedness to the nonlinear minimax maximum, and does not bias-correct the full-sample loss.
  • Prefix and subset stability (stability in postrank_diagnose()). Optionally re-solves the problem (requiring a solver, up to 12 single-thread solves) across MCMC draw prefixes or chain_subsets or across perturbed settings_grid entries (on IID inputs, only full-draw settings_grid overrides are supported), keeping all successful and failed solve records for descriptive comparison.

Conditional fixed-sample IID bounds

Unlike pointwise MCSEs, postrank_iid_bounds() computes an analytic simultaneous error radius across all M=(n2)M = \binom{n}{2} unordered pairs on each criterion when draws are independent (regime = "iid") and the sample size was fixed in advance. Because standard postrank inputs are tie-free (eji,k=1−eij,ke_{ji,k} = 1 - e_{ij,k}), bounding one direction per unordered pair bounds both directions, giving 2M events per criterion (rather than 4M in a tie-permitting formulation; tie-aware mode does not provide these IID helpers). With positive criterion error budgets αk\alpha_k summing to at most α\alpha, the radius on criterion kk is

εk=min⁡{1,log⁡(2M/αk)2Sk}. \varepsilon_k = \min\!\left\{1, \sqrt{\frac{\log(2M / \alpha_k)}{2 S_k}}\right\}.

IID bounds and protection envelopes hold only under their stated conditions:

  • Every criterion uses equal-weight independent (regime = "iid") entity-joint draws (pairs and criteria need not be independent of one another; MCMC draws are rejected because ESS is not an IID sample size).
  • Draw counts SkS_k and allocations αk\alpha_k were fixed before sampling, with adaptive_stopping = FALSE (design records your declaration without verifying it externally).
  • The 1−α1 - \alpha coverage statement refers to repeated Monte Carlo sampling from the same fixed posterior—it is not a posterior probability that a grading is right and does not check model specification.
  • Formulas are evaluated in ordinary floating-point arithmetic without interval enclosures, so guarantee_issued and numerically_verified remain FALSE (guarantee_status = "conditional_bound").

For postrank_toy, the synthetic generator fixed S=32S = 32 and α1=α2=0.025\alpha_1 = \alpha_2 = 0.025 (α=0.05\alpha = 0.05) before sampling:

Write δw=∑k=1Kwkεk\delta_w = \sum_{k=1}^K w_k \varepsilon_k. On the simultaneous 1−α1 - \alpha event where every |êij,k−eij,k|≤εk|\widehat{e}_{ij,k} - e_{ij,k}| \le \varepsilon_k, the empirical weighted loss of any fixed grading with NN ordered pairs differs from its true posterior weighted loss by at most (N/M)δw(N / M) \delta_w. Moreover, over any non-empty grading family fixed before sampling (G_max or exact_G, with objective = "weighted" and q = NULL), a checked grading ĝ\widehat{g} with verified whole-problem solver gap η=U−L\eta = U - L has true posterior weighted-loss regret at most 2δw+η2 \delta_w + \eta. postrank_regret_bound() evaluates that bound on a matching postrank_fit (bounding loss regret, not equality of grade vectors, and rejecting raw, relative, or candidate-only gaps):

When a weighted problem carries empirical error budgets q (Êk(g)≤qkN(g)\widehat{E}_k(g) \le q_k N(g)), Monte Carlo error also affects which gradings appear feasible. postrank_protection_envelopes() constructs two shifted problems without running a solver:

  • inner_problem: tightened constraints Êk(g)≤(qk−εk)N(g)\widehat{E}_k(g) \le (q_k - \varepsilon_k) N(g);
  • outer_problem: relaxed constraints Êk(g)≤(qk+εk)N(g)\widehat{E}_k(g) \le (q_k + \varepsilon_k) N(g).

The signed coefficients qk∓εkq_k \mp \varepsilon_k are not clipped to (0, 1), and zero-weight protected criteria still use their own εk\varepsilon_k. When qk−εk<0q_k - \varepsilon_k < 0, no grading with N>0N > 0 can satisfy the inner row, so an infeasible inner_problem does not prove that the true qq-constrained problem is infeasible. Passing the envelope to postrank_protection_analysis() solves both problems sequentially (requiring an optimization solver such as highs or licensed Gurobi) and combines the checked inner upper bound (inner_risk_upper) with the whole-problem outer lower bound (outer_whole_problem_risk_lower—never substituting the inner lower bound) to report computed_value equal to inner_risk_upper - outer_whole_problem_risk_lower + 2 * delta_w (status = "diagnostic_only", guarantee_issued = FALSE). See Lee (2026), Chapter 21, Effects of coefficient error on risk and feasibility, and Appendix D, Numerical helpers and their conditions.

Separating numerical precision from model adequacy

Solver gaps bound optimization error on the numbers passed to the solver, and MCSEs or IID radii describe how closely finite simulation draws approximate your supplied posterior. Neither quantity measures whether your statistical model matches reality: a tiny solver gap and a tiny MCSE can still accompany a misspecified likelihood or prior. See Lee (2026), Chapter 24, Self-reported error and calibration.

In plug-in empirical Bayes, posterior probabilities condition on an estimated prior distribution Ĝ\widehat{G} (not to be confused with the grade count GG); drawing more posterior samples while holding Ĝ\widehat{G} fixed does not propagate uncertainty in estimating Ĝ\widehat{G}. Hierarchical models integrate over the hyperparameter uncertainty specified by their prior, yet external calibration and measurement validity still require substantive model checking outside postrank; see Lee (2026), Chapter 24, Hyperparameters, precision and represented uncertainty.
