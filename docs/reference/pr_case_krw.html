Race and Gender Contact Gaps at 97 Employers

pr_case_krw.Rd

A compact teaching case adapted from Kline, Rose, and Walters' 2024 replication archive for A Discrimination Report Card. The primary input contains all 97 firms, with signed race and gender contact-gap targets. Larger race_signed means a larger white-name contact advantage, log(p_white) - log(p_black); larger gender_signed means a larger male-name contact advantage, log(p_male) - log(p_female). Signed gender is not the magnitude of a gap in either direction.

Usage

pr_case_krw

Format

A plain list with five elements:

input

A validated postrank_probabilities object with a 97 x 97 x 2 directional-error array and matching 97 x 2 cell-law means and 90% interval endpoints.

units

A 97-row table in input order: entity_id, firm_id, firm_name, industry, race_estimate, race_se, gender_estimate, and gender_se. Estimates and SEs are on the source log-contact-gap scale; they are not posterior means or standard deviations.

alternatives

Supported probability inputs named race, gender, and absolute_gender. The first two have K = 1; the last retains signed race and replaces signed gender with the absolute target.

reference

Archived actions, settings and law-labelled metrics; separate raw and plotting Pi arrays; cell-versus-archive diagnostics; and the limited source certification counts. These are reference records rather than current postrank_fit objects.

provenance

Relative source filenames and SHA-256 hashes, original grid component hashes, source versions, posterior interpretation, attribution, rights notice, and the maintainer extraction script.

Source

Kline, Patrick, Evan Rose, and Christopher Walters (2024). Data and Code for: A Discrimination Report Card. American Economic Association (publisher); ICPSR (distributor). doi:10.3886/E198284V1 . Extracted by data-raw/make-pr-case-krw.R from the frozen research case, preserving the original firm order and fixed posterior parameters. Original grids remain in the source archive and are not duplicated in this object.

Details

The probabilities and marginal summaries describe continuous uniform cells between adjacent retained posterior-grid midpoints. Each cell keeps its posterior mass, and the first race cell has nonnegative support. The empirical-Bayes prior and its fitted parameters remain fixed. There is no refit or Gaussian posterior approximation. The supplied means and central equal-tail intervals describe the same cell law; the interval level is 90%, so use level = .9 when requesting a report card. No target draws, Monte Carlo sample size, or cross-criterion joint law are supplied.

Single-criterion alternatives retain the same signed targets and firms. The absolute-gender alternative folds the original signed uniform cells, including cells crossing zero, and has its own probabilities and summaries. Raw archive Pi, its explicitly repaired plotting convention, and literal grid atoms are distinct references. They are not substituted for the primary cell-law input, and their numerical qualifications are not inherited by a new calculation.

The archived single-criterion cards use lambda = .25, corresponding to tau = .2. The archived common-grade operating point instead uses tau = .25, equal criterion weights and at most four grades. Stored actions are re-evaluated under the cell law without claiming a new optimum. The source research campaign completed 9 of 236 distinct numerical problems; the other 227 were not run. The archive describes the original correspondence experiment, not firms' current behavior or a causal policy comparison.

The source README states: "The data are licensed under a Creative Commons/CC-BY-NC license." It does not specify a license version. The compact adaptation retains that noncommercial source notice. These data conditions are separate from the package code license. The cell-law interpretation and compact extraction are adaptations by the postrank case-study maintainers; no endorsement by the original authors is implied.
