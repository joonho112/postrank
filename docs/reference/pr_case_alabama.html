Historical Economic Opportunity in 67 Alabama Counties

pr_case_alabama.Rd

A compact example combining income and incarceration outcomes for children born in 1978–1983, at parent income percentile 25, with race and gender pooled. Income ranks use household incomes from 2014–2015; incarceration is measured on April 1, 2010. The example favors higher income and lower incarceration. It describes historical conditional outcomes, not current county conditions or the causal effect of a policy.

Usage

pr_case_alabama

Format

A named list with five elements:

input

A validated postrank_probabilities input for all 67 counties and both criteria, from selected numerical pool A. It includes genuine marginal posterior means and fixed 95% equal-tail intervals.

units

67 rows in input order, with county IDs and names, published estimates and standard errors, oriented point scales, and source fields. The published population count is descriptive; it is not a binomial denominator for the fitted model.

alternatives

pool_B supplies probabilities from a separate numerical run; income and incarceration are one-criterion inputs from pool A. All retain their same-target marginal summaries.

reference

Archived settings, seven grade vectors, A/B evaluations, single-criterion solver results, commonality comparisons, and numerical precision information. These were computed with postrank 0.9.0 and HiGHS 1.12.0.3; they are not fresh fits by the installed package.

provenance

Source-relative filenames and hashes, outcome dates, directions, model conditioning, attribution and CC BY 4.0 notice.

Source

Porter (2022), Replication Data for: The Opportunity Atlas: Mapping the Childhood Roots of Social Mobility, Harvard Dataverse V1.0, doi:10.7910/DVN/NKCQM1 . Source: Opportunity Insights and U.S. Census Bureau. Selected CSV: https://opportunityinsights.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/county_outcomes_simple.csv. Observations and documented derivatives are available under CC BY 4.0. Changes include Alabama filtering, county identifiers and names, point scales, incarceration orientation, separate M1 posterior summaries, and compact package inputs. Attribution and earlier change notices are retained in provenance$rights. No endorsement is implied.

Details

The two criteria were fitted separately using the published conditional estimates and total standard errors, with Gaussian observation models and logistic-normal partial pooling. Their comparison probabilities support additive expected loss; they do not supply a joint posterior across criteria. The retained probabilities are Monte Carlo estimates, including observed zeros and ones, not exact posterior-certainty statements.

A and B use the same observations and model. They are separate numerical pools, not independent observational replications. Pool A uses 16,000 retained draws per criterion and pool B uses 8,000 in the source study; those draws are not included here and the supplied inputs do not claim retained samples or newly computed diagnostics. B failed the source's sensitive-pair MCSE target of .005 in 486 directional criterion rows (maximum .00586708). Its fixed-action risk precision was assessed separately. The archived MCSEs came from the original chains; they cannot be recalculated from these probability summaries. Constant-trace pair MCSE is unavailable.

The archived common grades use weights .5/.5, withholding cost .20, and at most five grades. Their grade vector equals the expected-rank cut baseline B3 in this example. B3 optimizes within a fixed score order; the common optimization's lower bound has a different scope. Numerical solver gaps do not measure Monte Carlo error or posterior uncertainty.

To use the supplied marginal intervals in postrank() or postrank_report_card(), request level = .95; intervals at other levels cannot be recovered from the stored summaries. A negative incarceration target has higher priority when original incarceration is lower. This direction differs from the source project's later policy-v2 needs analysis, which is not included.
