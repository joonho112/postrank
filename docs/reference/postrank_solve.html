Find Grades and Report Their Optimization Bounds

postrank_solve.Rd

A grading is sought within the family specified by postrank_problem(). Returned grades must satisfy an independent feasibility check. The fit retains the candidate, its directly recomputed posterior risk, the reported lower bound and the termination status. These records distinguish the available action from the numerical conclusions about optimality.

Usage

postrank_solve(problem, backend = NULL, control = list(), strategy = NULL)

Arguments

problem

A postrank_problem specifying the supplied probabilities, loss, grade limit and any supported error-budget or exact-count constraints.

backend

NULL (default), "gurobi" or "highs". An explicit choice takes precedence; otherwise getOption("postrank.backend") is used, then Gurobi if unset. The chosen name and source ("explicit", "option" or "default") are recorded in fit$backend. HiGHS must be chosen explicitly or through the option. A missing, failed or timed-out backend is never replaced automatically.

control

A postrank_control() object or named list of time, thread, seed, gap and tolerance controls. engine contains staged-search options. Package progress messages and native solver logs have separate switches.

strategy

NULL uses control$strategy, or "staged" if absent. An explicit "staged" or "label_mip" overrides the control object's choice.

Value

A postrank_fit. incumbent_validated identifies whether grade and evaluation are available; solution_validation retains the independent check. status contains raw_code, raw_message, normalized and termination_reason. objective contains primary_action_risk, risk_upper_bound, risk_lower_bound, risk_absolute_gap, raw objective values and their conversion, and the bound_source. problem, settings, backend, engine, warnings and numerical issues preserve the calculation's context. Minimax refinement adds minimax_refine.

Candidate feasibility and optimization bounds

A validated candidate's recomputed primary risk is the upper bound U. A usable lower bound L concerns the whole declared problem; the absolute risk gap is U-L. For minimax, U is the grade's largest included criterion risk, not a possibly slack auxiliary variable. A weighted raw gap must be converted using R = tau + Z/M; a raw relative gap is not the same as a relative gap in risk.

optimal is a numerical conclusion subject to recorded gap controls and tolerances. A backend's optimal label becomes feasible_not_certified if a valid candidate lacks a usable bound or has an excessive gap. A time_limit result may or may not contain a validated grade. With no validated grade, action risk and gap are unavailable, even if a useful lower bound remains. This is different from a valid one-grade report withholding all comparisons.

Inconsistent bounds can be rejected while independently validated grades remain available. Small bound inversions within the recorded objective tolerance may instead be clamped with a warning. Inspect bound_source, numerical issues and warnings; these are not exact numerical certificates, posterior uncertainty intervals, or proof of a unique optimal grade.

Search strategies

"label_mip" sends the integer-label model to the selected backend. "staged" first constructs candidates using contiguous cuts of several priority orders and local search. It then solves a linear relaxation with comparison constraints, including separated triangle inequalities and disclosure-count bounds implied by the grade limits. This can bound the full problem even though the initial candidate searches use fixed orders. If a gap remains and the size/settings permit, it runs a strengthened label MIP. Gurobi can use the candidate as a MIP start.

Staged-search defaults in control$engine are local_search_passes = 20, local_search_starts = 5, max_cut_rounds = 60, cuts_per_round = NULL (meaning max(200, 10 * n)), lp_time_share = .5, mip = "auto" and mip_max_n = 200. mip may also be "always" or "never"; "auto" runs the MIP only up to mip_max_n. With a possible MIP stage, the LP gets lp_time_share of the remaining budget. Otherwise it can use the remainder less a reserve for candidate construction and validation: the largest of one second, 1% of the time limit and twice the initial candidate-stage time. It receives at least the usual LP share. fit$engine$stages records what ran, including the LP time_budget and time_budget_rule.

Interpretation of minimax refinement

With minimax_refine = "weighted", the first stage minimizes the largest selected criterion risk. A second stage minimizes the original weighted risk while retaining the first-stage criterion caps. If any weight is zero, a third stage minimizes uniform criterion risk while also retaining a cap on the original weighted objective. No weight perturbation is used. Centered/scaled internal calculations retain the original public risk units.

Stage 1 receives three quarters of the time budget. Refinement shares the remainder, with at least one second in total after stage 1; with zero weights, stage 2 initially receives half and stage 3 the time left. Heuristic fallbacks share their stage's deadline. Construction and validation add overhead, so time_limit is not a strict wall-clock bound. The final primary status and lower bound come from stage 1 and are checked against the released action. A candidate that breaches primary caps or worsens the original weighted objective beyond machine rounding is excluded.

fit$minimax_refine records stage results, budgets and the released action. lexicographic_certified requires primary and original-weighted certification for that action; pareto_certified also requires the zero-weight third stage when applicable. These flags remain subject to solver gaps and floating-point tolerances. Skipped or uncertified refinement is reported with a warning when a first-stage action is available. minimax_refine = "none" keeps the first-stage action. fit$backend$options retains the first-stage options.

Examples

problem <- postrank_problem(pr_tutorial$one, weights = c(score_A = 1),
  tau = .2, G_max = 3L)
if (requireNamespace("highs", quietly = TRUE)) {
  fit <- postrank_solve(problem, backend = "highs",
    control = postrank_control(time_limit = 10))
  fit$status$normalized
  fit$objective[c("risk_lower_bound", "risk_upper_bound", "risk_absolute_gap")]
}

#> [1] 0
